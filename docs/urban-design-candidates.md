## Candidates

### 1. Adyar Basin Vision Framework — Chennai

**PDF pages 2–5** · Professional (Sponge Collaborative) · Role: Project Architect and expert
stakeholder facilitator · Team of six.

An integrated framework tying urban growth priorities to ecologically sensitive development
across a river basin spanning urban, peri-urban, and rural transects. Six zones, ten goals
grouped across urban ecology, risk and resilience, culture and livelihoods, open space and
access, and housing. The owner developed transect-level strategies, ran the expert
stakeholder workshop, built the zone-strategy evaluation matrix, and did the GIS work.

- **Proposed tier:** flagship candidate — it is the largest-scale piece of work in the set
  and the one that best shows framework thinking.
- **Strongest visuals:** the basin hydrological-cycle diagrams (natural vs urbanised), the
  infrastructure and risk thematic map, the zone-wise vision plate.
- **Approval question:** the vision plate is marked with an asterisk in the PDF, which
  usually flags a team or client-restricted image. Confirm what may be shown.

### 2. Mapping Critical Flood Plains, Woodbridge Township, NJ

**PDF pages 6–8** · Academic (Rutgers, Prof. Will Payne) · Individual project.

Flood vulnerability of a New Jersey township, focused on the Watson-Crampton neighbourhood
(~215 acres) after Superstorm Sandy. The state's Blue Acres programme removed roughly 1,300
households from flood-affected areas and rezoned 120 acres from residential to open space
conservation; the analysis finds **163 land parcels still in threat of future flooding**.

- **Proposed tier:** standard. This one is unambiguous — academic, individual, no client.
- **Cross-listing:** it is genuinely a geospatial project too, and it is the direct
  ancestor of the NJ parcel flood risk dashboard. Worth saying so explicitly.
- **Approval question:** none. **Recommended as the first project to publish in this
  section.**

### 3. Impact of Proximity to Pedestrian-Heavy Areas on Crash Rates — Midtown Manhattan

**PDF pages 9–10** · Certification project (NEBDHub & NSDC Transportation Data Science).

Whether proximity to schools, parks, and community centres predicts pedestrian and cyclist
KSI crashes, using NYC OpenData collisions with 1,000-foot buffers around pedestrian
generators. The résumé records 11 high-risk intersections identified and recommendations to
NYCDOT.

- **Proposed tier:** gallery, or standard if you want the transport-safety thread visible.
- **Note:** this is arguably a geospatial project rather than an urban design one. Say
  where you want it.

### 4. Integrated Sponge City Approach, Kosasthalaiyar Basin — Chennai

**PDF pages 11–12** · Professional (Greater Chennai Corporation) · Role: Project Architect,
design and technical lead.

Feasibility of blue-green infrastructure and nature-based solutions to increase the
resilience of grey stormwater infrastructure, across a ~170-acre study area, with a detailed
project report for a pilot site in Mathur MMDA Colony. Runoff coefficients and dynamic
modelling in EPA SWMM quantify the impact.

- **Proposed tier:** flagship candidate — this is the piece with real hydrological modelling
  behind it, which is the bridge between this section and the flood work.
- **Strongest visuals:** the flooding-nodes model output, the BGI intervention framework,
  the street-typology intervention catalogue.
- **Approval question:** client work for a municipal corporation. Confirm publication.

### 5. Kilambakkam Climate Interpretation Park — Chennai

**PDF pages 13–15** · Professional (Chennai Metropolitan Development Authority).

A climate interpretation park demonstrating native landscapes and blue-green infrastructure
as a public-education instrument, conceived as an exemplary sponge open space. Documented
strategies for ecology, planting (**~6,500 plants across 268 species**, selected for
hardiness, indigeneity, biological resistance, and non-invasiveness), and low-impact grading.

- **Proposed tier:** standard.
- **Strongest visuals:** the landscape character zoning, the archaeological interpretation
  centre view, the retention pond views.
- **Approval question:** client work for a development authority. Confirm publication.

### 6. Telangana State Urban Center of Excellence

**PDF pages 16–19** · Competition · Finalist, NIUA (2021).

An urban campus for research, start-up incubation, and capacity building, organised as five
districts — residential, mixed-use innovation, infrastructure, cultural, and an urban
ecological preserve — with green corridors and integrating loops. Includes waste-to-compost,
greywater phytoremediation, and an on-site energy plant.

- **Proposed tier:** standard. Competition work is normally safe to publish; confirm the
  team credit line.
- **Strongest visual:** the axonometric of the whole campus.

### 7. An Urban Theater — Third Space in Urban Neighbourhoods

**PDF pages 20–23** · Academic · Undergraduate thesis, Anna University · Nominated for
ARCASIA Thesis of the Year and the COA National Awards.

Third spaces as a spatial counterpoint to home and work, organised around the 15-minute life
circle and the relationship between transit space and city life. Interventions are staged
across urban, architectural, micro, temporal, and portable scales — that ladder is the
interesting part.

- **Proposed tier:** standard. Individual academic work, award-nominated, no client.
- **Approval question:** none.

### 8. Marutha Nagar Bio-Park — Coimbatore

**PDF pages 24–25** · Professional (Coimbatore City Municipal Corporation, from
"Reimagining OSRs for a Climate Resilient Coimbatore").

A pilot sponge open space demonstrating how open space reservation land can be programmed as
multifunctional blue, green, and social infrastructure, with aquifer recharge as the
organising idea.

- **Proposed tier:** gallery, unless you want it paired with candidate 4 as a
  "pilot projects" cluster.
- **Approval question:** client work. Confirm publication.
- **Note:** the PDF labels these pages with the thesis credit block, which appears to be a
  layout error carried from the previous spread. Worth correcting in any re-export.

### 9. Affordable housing in smart city planning

**PDF page 26** · Academic (Prof. Meenakumari).

Research on housing development capacity and affordability for EWS/LIG households under
high urbanisation and migration pressure, and the enabling framework government would need
to provide.

- **Proposed tier:** gallery. One page in the source; there may be more material elsewhere.
- **Note:** this is the only housing-policy piece in the set, and housing is a live thread
  in the current CLiME work. It may be worth more space than the PDF gives it.

### 10. Buckingham Canal Waterfront — Chennai

**PDF pages 27–28** · Public space proposal.

The canal edge as a unifying element between local and central systems, with a set of
tactical interventions: street fitness, a rainbow pavilion, a pop-up market, a kids' play
street.

- **Proposed tier:** gallery. Lighter than the rest, but it is the only tactical-urbanism
  piece and it shows a different register.

### 11. Integrated Lakefront Development — Chennai (Velachery and three other lakes)

**PDF pages 29–31** · Competition · **Winner, Chennai Lakefront Rejuvenation Competition,
CMDA (2023)**.

A three-step framework — restore, connect, engage — for lakes threatened by sewage, waste,
encroachment, and unplanned development. Velachery lakefront is positioned as resilient
community infrastructure combining flood mitigation with circular-economy programming in
urban agriculture, waste management, and street vending.

- **Proposed tier:** flagship candidate. It is the competition **win**, it covers four
  lakes, and the restore/connect/engage framing travels well.
- **Approval question:** the four-lake vision plate is asterisked in the PDF. Confirm what
  may be shown and how the team should be credited.
