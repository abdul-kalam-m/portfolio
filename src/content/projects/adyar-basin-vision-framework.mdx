## Problem

The Adyar is the southernmost of three major river systems running through Chennai, and its
basin spans urban, peri-urban, and rural contexts in a single transect. Urbanization has
been quietly rewriting the basin's hydrology: encroached waterbodies, riparian edges built
up to the water line, contaminated recharge, and rising flood risk where a natural system
used to absorb runoff. The basin needed a framework that could hold both a long-term
planning horizon and short-term pilot projects in the same document — one that a
municipality could actually act on.

## Approach

As project architect and expert stakeholder facilitator, I developed transect-level
strategies for the basin, organized and ran the expert stakeholder workshop, reviewed
zone-wise strategies, and built the evaluation matrix that scored them. The team collected,
analyzed, and visualized the GIS datasets — watersheds, wetlands, water infrastructure, and
land cover — that the framework is built on.

The ten development goals are grouped under five themes — urban ecology, risk and
resilience, culture and livelihoods, open space and access, and urban development and
housing — and mapped onto six zones, each with its own tailored strategy. A zone with high
flood risk prioritizes water retention and storage; a zone near Guindy National Park
prioritizes conservation-compatible development. The strategies aren't generic best
practice restated per zone — they follow from an infrastructure-and-risk thematic map built
specifically for this basin.

## Results

The vision resolves into a single continuous move: an eco-loop that threads Guindy National
Park's conservation core, "sponge streets" that let stormwater infiltrate rather than run
off, a resilient riparian edge along the river itself, and heritage-district conservation
where the river meets the city's older fabric — ending at a protected estuary and sandbar
at the coast.

## What I'd do differently

The framework is strong on ecological and infrastructural logic but light on an explicit
phasing and financing sequence — the kind of detail a municipality needs to move from
vision to procurement. A companion capital-improvement schedule, zone by zone, would have
made the framework immediately actionable rather than aspirational.
