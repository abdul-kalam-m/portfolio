## Problem

Chennai's lakes — Velachery, Adambakkam, Perumbakkam, and Madambakkam among them — are
threatened by sewage, waste, encroachment, and unplanned development, even though they sit
at the center of the Chennai Metropolitan Area's southward growth along the GST and OMR
corridors. Treating them as leftover water bodies rather than infrastructure was the
problem; the competition brief asked for a systems approach that could rejuvenate all four
as one connected asset rather than four separate cleanup projects.

## Approach

As design assistant, visualization lead, and analytics coordinator, I led 3D massing in
SketchUp for perspective collages and detailed axonometric views, and supported the
analysis team in validating the technical datasets — urban growth, street network, transit
and walkability, topography, natural drainage, and green cover — that the framework is
built on.

The proposal organizes every intervention under three verbs: **restore**, by improving
water quality, storage capacity, and groundwater infiltration and providing habitat for
native species; **connect**, by making the lakefront publicly accessible and linked to
sustainable mobility; and **engage**, by ensuring the lakes' sustainability through
community buy-in, local stewardship, and culturally meaningful interaction with water.

## Results

Velachery Lakefront is the framework's pilot — positioned as resilient community
infrastructure that mitigates flooding through stormwater management while advancing a
circular economy through waste, food, and energy management, integrating urban agriculture,
solid-waste management, and street vending into the same lakefront promenade.

## What I'd do differently

The framework is explicit about Phase I at Velachery but doesn't state how the other three
lakes sequence relative to it or to each other. A cross-lake prioritization — by flood risk
reduction per rupee, the same logic the Adyar Basin evaluation matrix used — would turn four
independent lake plans into one investable programme.
